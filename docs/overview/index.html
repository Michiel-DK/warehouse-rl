What we built

A warehouse where we plant the faults ourselves.

A simulator with 80 bad floor cells hidden on the aisles. The analyser gets only the robot messages, never the answer, so every call it makes can be checked.

Step 2 · who is stuck

Names the robot, the spot and a likely reason.

30 seconds waiting with a job means stuck. Then: a broken robot in the way, another stuck one, traffic, or nothing at all.

Step 4 · the operator's list

Plain sentences. Each one checked afterwards.

Rules over the counts, no language model. A cell that holds robots up far more than the rest, a robot stuck far more than its peers, two robots that keep blocking each other.

What's next

Give the map to the route planner.

Robots steer around the worst cells. On a floor that traps robots, the map wins back part of the lost pick-rate.

11.1%of the pick-rate lost to the bad floor won back with the analyser's map95% interval 6.0% to 15.9%. With perfect knowledge: 26.9%, interval 22.4% to 31.4%.
38%fewer drives into bad cells with the map on
Tested, weakly. On mild floor damage the gain is within noise; on damage that traps robots it is measurable.

Order lines per hour, mean of 30 shifts. The bars come from a test on a floor that traps robots, not from the shift above. Bars start at zero; the line is the 95% interval.

Map alpha 32 hurts at p 0.3-0.7 (share -92%), is within noise at p 0.6-0.9 (share -10%, interval -23% to 1%) and helps at p 0.8-0.95 (share 15%) and p 0.9-0.99 (share 11%).

No map setting beats the oracle beyond noise: map alpha 4 at p 0.6-0.9 has the higher point share (16% against 14%) but the intervals overlap.
